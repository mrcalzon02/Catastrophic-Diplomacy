# Level System I — hundreds of replayable wave battles

**Design goal (partially implemented and playable):** a 500-level initial campaign comprising ten sectors of fifty stages, each with increasingly demanding enemy waves, distinct doctrines, sustained ammunition attrition, sector bosses and optional replay grinding. Preserve Sandbox and the deterministic simulation as independent modes. Do not conflate this with a strategy layer.

Every level supplies its own fixed maximum deployment count C, base credits B and a wave roster. D is the number of commissioned vessels deployed (1..C); S is the number of those vessels that survive the **entire level** (0..D). Fighters, defensive drones and expendables do not increase D or S.

**Victory reward:**

`credits = floor(B * (S / D) * (1.0 + 0.10 * (C - D)))`

One unused slot yields 1.1×, two 1.2× and so forth. The multiplier is an **additive 0.1 step per missing ship**, not exponential compounding. Enemy defeat/all mandatory waves must be confirmed before any award; defeat, abandonment or incomplete levels pay zero. **Updated economy rule:** first clears retain the exact full credit formula. Every successful repeat of a previously cleared mission pays **floor(full payout / 4)**. Replays are deliberately valuable chiefly as research time rather than unrestricted credit farming. This supersedes the original proposed full-payout replay policy. Failures grant neither credits nor research work.

Illustrative 20-capacity, 10,000-credit level: 20 deployed/20 survive = 10,000; 16/16 = 14,000; 10/10 = 20,000; 1/1 = 29,000; 16/12 = 10,500. Do not apply repairs or rearming between individual waves unless explicitly introduced as a scenario modifier. Vessel condition carries across waves within an attempt; each new replay starts from the chosen saved fleet design.

Research must be aggressively expensive to motivate refinement and grinding. Draft tier prices in credits: I 25,000; II 75,000; III 250,000; IV 900,000; V 3,500,000; VI 15,000,000. These are **not balanced or live prices**. Tie purchases to the existing 120 components / 2,880 technology upgrade catalog after verifying progression and save migration. Avoid treating ownership of a technology CSV entry as an already purchased upgrade.

Remaining development backlog: validated saved loadout choices between sessions; richer enemy composition and true sector bosses; per-level mission goals; technology purchase ledger tied to the actual component catalog; expanded after-action telemetry, difficulty/performance balancing and compatibility testing under the directional-protection model. Deterministic level definitions, multi-wave battles, chosen deployed fleet size, survivor rewards, a persistent campaign bank and basic results/replay navigation are implemented.

## First committed code foundation

`LevelEconomy.level(1..500)` now builds deterministic level metadata (ten 50-stage sectors, fixed per-level maximum fleet, seeded encounters, baseline rewards and initial wave counts). `victoryCredits` implements the exact integer-rational formula with overflow-checked credit output, invalid deployment rejection, and a zero payout for incomplete attempts. `draftResearchCost` supplies the six proposed prices. `LevelEconomyTest` covers the published 20-ship examples and progression boundaries. **Progress since the foundation:** the playable campaign menu, clickable 500-stage star map, checksum-protected credit/unlock save and after-action report now use this level economy. `CampaignEncounter` spawns sequential enemy reinforcement waves inside the same `Battle`; hull damage, ammunition and aircraft persist across waves, with reward and unlock only after the final wave. `CampaignDeployment` lets the player select 1..fleet-cap commissioned ships and a battle line, attack wedge or echelon formation; these are applied to actual friendly ship coordinates. The first scenario roster uses common opposing hull fittings and simple escalating reinforcement counts—not yet bespoke mission objectives, varied waves or sector bosses. **Six sequential paid technology licenses now require 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 5 successful mission work units. Four optional, expensive and campaign milestone-gated lab acceleration projects are also researched over 2–5 work units and each adds one research work per victorious mission, up to five per victory. Every success, including replays, advances the current project; failures do not. A full per-component laboratory purchase ledger is still pending.**

## Level backgrounds now live in the combat sandbox

The current 1..500 selector maps every level directly to `LevelSky.forLevel(level)` and `SpaceWeather.forLevel(level)`. The normal app starts in its **main menu**, not level 1 combat. Skirmishes can use **B/N** and touchable < / > LEVEL controls to change levels and restart an encounter with the updated sky and environmental conditions. **W** and tapping the ENV bar toggle hazardous weather in skirmishes, and **R** restarts the encounter without changing its selected environment. Campaign missions fix the environment according to their actual stage, and the in-battle HUD displays the active mission and wave. Rendering runs before the 200v200 combat grid and ships; VFX occurs behind and above hulls with a stationary HUD. The same environments now also run inside the playable multi-wave campaign. Android/Fire and Windows/Linux use the same deterministic simulation and deployment code.
